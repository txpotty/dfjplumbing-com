    Both bladder and diaphragm tanks use an air cushion to pressurize water, but they differ in how the water and air are separated. Bladder tanks have a replaceable rubber bladder that holds the water, completely isolating it from the air. Diaphragm tanks use a fixed rubber diaphragm. Bladder tanks are generally preferred for their longevity and ease of maintenance.

    While it might seem like a DIY project, well pressure tank replacement involves working with pressurized water, electrical components, and ensuring proper sizing and calibration.     Common signs include your well pump constantly turning on and off (short cycling), fluctuating water pressure, air sputtering from faucets, or a tank that is waterlogged (feels heavy when tapped and full of water). If you notice any of these, it is time for an inspection.
