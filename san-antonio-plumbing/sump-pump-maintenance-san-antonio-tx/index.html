    Yes, the hard water prevalent in many parts of Texas, including San Antonio, can affect your sump pump's lifespan. Mineral deposits from hard water can build up on the pump's impeller and in the pit, reducing efficiency and potentially causing clogs or damage over time. Regular maintenance helps mitigate these effects.

    The check valve is crucial because it prevents water that has been pumped out from flowing back into the sump pit after the pump shuts off. A faulty check valve can cause the pump to short cycle, leading to premature wear and increased energy consumption. Our maintenance includes checking its functionality.

    We recommend having your sump pump professionally maintained at least once a year. If your home is in an area prone to heavy rainfall or has a high water table, such as many parts of San Antonio, bi-annual maintenance might be more beneficial. Regular checks ensure it's ready for peak seasons.

    While you can perform basic checks like ensuring the pump is plugged in and the discharge pipe isn't blocked, professional maintenance goes much deeper.
