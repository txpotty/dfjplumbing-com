    Homeowner's insurance typically covers sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe. However, coverage for leaks caused by neglect or long-term issues like corrosion may vary. We recommend contacting your insurance provider directly to understand your policy's specific coverage for water leak repairs.

    To prevent future water leaks, consider regular plumbing inspections, insulating exposed pipes before winter freezes, maintaining proper water pressure, and addressing small drips promptly. Replacing old, corroded pipes with modern materials like PEX can also significantly reduce leak risks over time.

    In Texas, water pipes can leak due to several factors including age and corrosion, shifting clay soils that stress pipes, high water pressure, and occasional freezing temperatures that can cause pipes to burst. Hard water common in some areas can also contribute to scale buildup and eventual pipe degradation.

    While minor leaks like a dripping faucet might be a DIY fix, it's generally best to call a professional for most water leaks. Hidden leaks, especially in walls or under slabs, require specialized equipment for detection and proper repair to prevent further damage and ensure code compliance. Improper repairs can lead to more significant problems down the line.

    Common signs of a hidden water leak include unexplained spikes in your water bill, persistent sounds of running water when no fixtures are in use, visible water stains on walls or ceilings, musty odors, and soggy spots in your yard. For slab leaks, you might notice warm spots on your floor or cracks in your foundation.
