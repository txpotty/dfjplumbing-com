    The terms are often used interchangeably, but 'grease interceptor' typically refers to larger, outdoor units with capacities over 500 gallons, while 'grease trap' usually describes smaller, indoor units under 100 gallons, such as under-sink models.     Key signs include slow drains in your kitchen sink, gurgling noises from drains, foul odors emanating from your drains or around the trap area, and visible FOG buildup when you inspect the trap. Ignoring these can lead to complete blockages.

In grease traps, it separates from wastewater, but if not regularly removed, it overflows into the sewer system, causing blockages and environmental issues.
