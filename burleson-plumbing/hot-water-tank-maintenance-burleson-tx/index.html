    Anode Rod Inspection & Replacement in Burleson, TX

    The anode rod is your hot water tank's sacrificial protector against corrosion, and it's especially important with the mineral content often found in Texas water. During our maintenance, we pull and inspect the anode rod. If it's significantly depleted, we recommend and perform a replacement to extend the life of your tank in Burleson, TX, preventing rust from eating away at the tank lining.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Hot Water Tank Maintenance in Burleson

    The cost for hot water tank maintenance in Burleson, TX typically ranges from $100 to $250, depending on the tank type, age, and specific services required like anode rod replacement. We provide a detailed quote before any work begins.

    DFJ Plumbing services all major brands of hot water tanks, including but not limited to Rheem, Bradford White, A. O. Smith, State, Noritz, and Bosch. Our technicians are familiar with a wide range of models and their specific maintenance needs.

    Routine hot water tank maintenance, such as flushing and anode rod replacement, generally does not require a permit in Burleson, TX. However, if major repairs or a full replacement are needed, a permit may be required to ensure compliance with local building codes.

    Most manufacturers and plumbing experts recommend annual hot water tank maintenance. This is especially true in areas like Texas with hard water, which can accelerate sediment buildup and anode rod depletion.

    The anode rod, also known as a sacrificial rod, is a metal rod inside your hot water tank designed to corrode before the tank itself. It protects the tank lining from rust and corrosion, especially important with the mineral content in Texas water, extending the lifespan of your unit.

    A typical hot water tank maintenance service usually takes about 1 to 2 hours. This includes draining, flushing, inspection, and any minor adjustments or part replacements like an anode rod.

    Common signs include reduced hot water supply, strange noises (like rumbling or popping), rust-colored water, a sulfur or rotten egg smell, or water leaking from the tank. These indicate issues that maintenance can often resolve.

    Regular, professional maintenance can often be a condition for keeping your manufacturer's warranty valid. Check your specific warranty documentation, but generally, proof of annual service can help ensure your warranty remains intact.
