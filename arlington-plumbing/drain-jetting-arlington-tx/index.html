    Frequently Asked Questions About Drain Jetting in Arlington

    The cost of drain jetting in Arlington can range from $300 to $800, depending on the severity of the clog, the length and diameter of the pipe, and ease of access.     Drain snaking typically involves pushing an auger through a clog to create a small opening for water to pass. Drain jetting, or hydro jetting, uses high-pressure water to thoroughly scour the entire pipe wall, removing all buildup and leaving the pipe much cleaner. Jetting is more effective for stubborn blockages and preventative maintenance.

    We strongly advise against using a home pressure washer for drain jetting. These machines lack the necessary power, specialized nozzles, and safety features of professional equipment. Improper use can damage your pipes, cause severe injury, or push clogs deeper. Always rely on trained professionals for drain jetting.

    While drain jetting focuses on clearing existing pipes, we work with various reliable brands for replacement parts if needed, such as Charlotte Pipe for PVC, Cast Iron Soil Pipe Institute (CISPI) standards for cast iron, and various manufacturers for cleanout assemblies. Our focus is on the integrity of your existing drain system.

    Yes, when performed by experienced professionals, drain jetting is generally safe for most plumbing systems. Our technicians assess your pipe material and condition using camera inspections before jetting to ensure the appropriate water pressure is used. It is not recommended for severely damaged or corroded pipes.

    Generally, a permit is not required for routine drain jetting services as it is a maintenance activity within existing plumbing. However, if the jetting reveals significant pipe damage requiring excavation and repair or replacement, a plumbing permit from the city of Arlington may be necessary. Our team will advise you if such a situation arises.

    Signs you might need drain jetting include frequently recurring clogs, very slow drains in multiple fixtures, foul odors coming from drains, gurgling sounds, or sewage backups. If traditional snaking hasn't solved your issues, jetting is likely the next step.

    Yes, drain jetting is highly effective at removing tree roots that have infiltrated sewer lines.
